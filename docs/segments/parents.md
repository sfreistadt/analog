# Segment Deep Dive — Parents

← [Back to market brainstorm](../market-brainstorm.md)

---

## The Core Pain

Parents are caught in a specific bind: they feel guilty about their own screen time, anxious about their kids' screen time, and simultaneously too exhausted to plan something better. The intention-action gap is enormous. They know what they *should* be doing on a Saturday. They don't do it.

**The enemy isn't desire — it's friction and decision fatigue.**

Planning a family outing requires: deciding what to do, checking if kids will tolerate it, finding options, reading reviews, checking availability, booking, managing logistics, and getting everyone out the door. By 9am Saturday that's already too much.

---

## Who Specifically

### Primary: The Coordinator Parent (30–45)
Usually but not always the mom. Runs the family calendar. Already subscribes to things (meal kits, activity boxes, sports leagues) as outsourced planning. Familiar with the "we paid, we're going" commitment device. Responds to curation.

- Household income: $100k+
- Kids: 4–14 (old enough to do things, young enough to still want to)
- Already feels behind on "enriching" activities
- Follows accounts like "things to do in [city]" on Instagram but never acts on them

### Secondary: The Reluctant Screen Parent (35–50)
Knows the research. Genuinely worried. Has tried screen time limits and failed. Looking for a structural solution — something that fills the void rather than just restricts the bad thing. Would frame this purchase as parenting, not leisure.

---

## What They Actually Want

Not just an activity — a **ready-made plan** they can hand to the family. The magic words: *"This Saturday, we're doing this."*

- Zero research required
- Age-appropriate
- Within a reasonable drive (or walk)
- Something the kids won't immediately reject
- Feels like a good parent move, not just entertainment

**Bonus if:** there's a story attached ("we went to a working farm"), it's off-screen by design, or it creates a family tradition feeling.

---

## The Format We're Testing

→ See [Scavenger Hunt concept](../concepts/scavenger-hunt.md)

The scavenger hunt solves something a marketplace doesn't: **the product is the experience**, not just a pointer to one. The phone gets you started, then you put it down.

---

## Business Model Options

### 1. Free first hunt, paid library
One free neighborhood hunt to prove the concept, then $5–8/hunt or $12/month subscription.

### 2. Weekend Dispatch (Subscription)
Weekly email/SMS: *"This Saturday in [city]: here's your family plan."* One curated hunt, pre-built for your area. $15–25/month.

### 3. Gift Product
"Give the gift of an analog weekend." Grandparents, aunts/uncles buy hunts for families. Removes the self-purchase barrier. $25–40/kit.

### 4. School / Library Licensing
Bulk hunts for field trips, after-school programs. $200–500/school/year.

---

## Pricing Intuition

- Subscription: $12–25/month (less than one bad DoorDash order)
- Per-hunt: $5–8 (impulse buy, easy yes)
- Gift kit: $25–40 (holiday, birthday, teacher gift)
- Family per-experience: $50–150 (comparable to dinner + movie)

---

## Acquisition Angles

- **Mom Facebook groups** — highest density of target buyer, word of mouth is currency
- **Pediatricians / child therapists** — referral channel for the screen-time-anxious parent
- **School newsletters** — trusted, low cost if you can get placement
- **Parenting newsletters** (Motherly, Fatherly, Big Life Journal audience)
- **Instagram/TikTok** — content showing the *before and after* of an analog Saturday

---

## Biggest Unknown

**Is the pain "I don't know what to do" or "I know what to do but won't commit"?**

If discovery → curated list with good UX wins.
If commitment → paid, time-stamped, non-refundable booking wins.

These are different products. Need interviews to find out which unlocks action.

---

## Risks

- **Seasonality** — outdoor experiences crater in extreme heat (Austin summers are brutal)
- **Kid veto power** — even great plans get blown up if a 7-year-old says no
- **Supply side** — need enough quality hunts per city before the product has value
- **Competition from free** — "things to do with kids in Austin" is a Google search away
