# Concept — The Outdoor Scavenger Hunt

← [Back to brief](../brief.md) | [Parents segment](../segments/parents.md)

---

## The Shift

Moving away from a marketplace that points families *toward* experiences — toward a product that **is** the experience. The platform's job is to hand the family a structured outdoor adventure, then get out of the way.

**Key insight:** The scavenger hunt format enforces the analog behavior by design. You can't complete it on a couch. The clues require the physical world — talking to people, observing things, touching things, going places.

---

## How It Works

1. Parent visits the site, enters their city/neighborhood and kids' ages
2. Gets a tailored hunt — a set of clues, challenges, and stops designed for that area
3. Prints it out (or gets a one-time SMS drip) — phone goes in the bag
4. Family goes outside and does the thing
5. Optional: photo at the end, submitted to unlock the next hunt

**The product lives on a screen for 90 seconds. The experience lives outside for 2–3 hours.**

---

## What Makes a Great Hunt

### The clues must require the physical world
- "Find a building that was built before your parents were born — what does the sign say?"
- "Ask someone who works outside what the best part of their job is"
- "Find something in nature that is exactly the color of your front door"
- "Collect 5 things from the ground. Make the smallest possible sculpture. Leave it for the next person."

### The format scales by age
- **Ages 4–7:** simple observation, colors, counting, animals
- **Ages 8–12:** reading, mild problem-solving, talking to strangers (with parent), light navigation
- **Ages 13+:** more challenge, autonomy, maybe a mystery narrative arc

### Locations that work well
- Neighborhood walks (lowest barrier — no driving)
- Parks and greenways
- Downtown / main street (talk to shopkeepers, read historical markers)
- Nature trails
- Farmers markets

### Every hunt needs a payoff moment
Not just completion — *a moment*. Something to talk about at dinner. A weird discovery, a conversation with a stranger, something they made or found.

## Format Options

### A — Printable PDF ← test this first
A beautifully designed, printable adventure sheet. Seasonal. Neighborhood-specific. Print and go.

- ✅ Truly off-screen once you leave the house
- ✅ Zero cost to produce
- ✅ Feels like an artifact, giftable
- ❌ Requires a printer
- ❌ Static, no personalization after print

### B — SMS Drip
Parent texts a number to start. Clues arrive one at a time — next clue only unlocks when you reply. Phone stays in pocket between clues.

- ✅ No app needed, extremely low tech
- ✅ Feels like a game
- ❌ SMS costs add up
- ❌ Harder to design

### C — Minimal Web App (clue-gated)
Simple mobile web page. One clue at a time. Submit an answer to unlock the next. Used in 10-second bursts, not scrolled.

- ✅ Personalization possible, tracks completion
- ✅ Easiest to iterate
- ❌ Still a screen
- ❌ Needs data signal

### D — Physical Envelope Kit (shipped)
A box arrives: folded map, numbered envelopes, small props (magnifying glass, field notebook, crayon). Open each envelope only when you reach the location.

- ✅ Premium feel, genuinely zero-screen, highly giftable
- ❌ Expensive to produce and ship
- ❌ One-time use, hard to scale

## The Version to Test First

**Neighborhood walk hunt · Printable PDF · Ages 6–12 · Austin**

- Zero cost to produce
- Parent downloads and prints
- 90-minute walk from home
- 10–15 clues mixing observation, collection, conversation, and creation
- Ends with a family debrief: 3 questions to answer over snacks

Ship one hunt. Give it to 5 families. Watch what happens.

---

## What This Is Not

- Not a points/leaderboard/achievement system (gamification = more screen time thinking)
- Not a live GPS tracker ("you are here" removes the discovery)
- Not social sharing baked into the core
- Not a subscription content treadmill in v1
