# Addressable Market Brainstorm

> Who is suffering most from over-digitization, has money, and would pay to feel more human?

---

## Market Segments

### 1. Burnt-out professionals (25–45)
Knowledge workers — tech, finance, consulting — who are screen-saturated by profession. They have disposable income, recognize the problem, and are primed to pay for a solution that feels like a deliberate lifestyle choice rather than a diet.

**Experience types they want:** cooking classes, hiking groups, pottery, live music, board game nights, volunteer days
**Willingness to pay:** High ($30–100/experience)
**Pain:** Friction of finding things + social pressure to stay online

---

### 2. Parents trying to model analog behavior ← current focus
Parents who are worried about their own and their kids' screen time. They want structured offline family activities but planning fatigue is real — they'll pay for curation.

**Experience types:** outdoor adventures, maker workshops, family farm days, pick-your-own, cooking together
**Willingness to pay:** Medium-high (family spend framing, $50–150/outing)
**Pain:** Discovery + logistics + commitment device ("we paid, we're going")

→ See [Parents deep dive](segments/parents.md)

---

### 3. Retirees and empty nesters (60+)
Underserved by tech products by design. Motivated by health, loneliness reduction, and purpose. Have the most free time and often the most wealth. Don't want a complicated app — want a phone call or a postcard.

**Experience types:** garden clubs, walking groups, art classes, volunteer programs, travel
**Willingness to pay:** Medium ($20–60/experience or subscription)
**Pain:** Isolation + unclear how to find like-minded people + tech friction

---

### 4. Gen Z backlash cohort (18–28)
A growing segment actively rejecting the attention economy — dumb phones, film cameras, cash-only. Early adopters of analog identity. Influential but lower income.

**Experience types:** zine fests, vinyl listening sessions, darkroom photography, urban foraging, live storytelling
**Willingness to pay:** Lower individually ($10–30) but high viral/brand value
**Pain:** Finding others like them + legitimacy/curation

---

### 5. Corporate wellness buyers (B2B)
HR teams at companies with return-to-office or burnout mandates. Buying offline experiences as employee benefits — team building, mental health days, quarterly offsites.

**Experience types:** facilitated group experiences, skills workshops, outdoor challenges
**Willingness to pay:** Very high ($150–500/person as a budget line)
**Pain:** Sourcing vetted experiences at scale + reporting/receipts for HR

---

### 6. Therapists and mental health providers
Prescribing "experience homework" — behavioral activation, nature therapy, social reconnection. Could be a referral channel or a clinical B2B product.

**Experience types:** structured outdoor activities, community volunteering, creative expression
**Willingness to pay:** Medium (reimbursement model potential)
**Pain:** Nothing exists to refer patients to; current recs are totally unstructured

---

## Wedge Hypotheses

| Segment | Wedge | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Burnt-out professionals | Curated solo experiences via SMS | High WTP, clear pain, no commitment required |
| **Parents** | **Family scavenger hunt** | Planning fatigue is acute; product IS the experience |
| Corporate wellness | Offsite experience marketplace | B2B revenue, high ACV, less price sensitivity |
| Gen Z | Analog social club, city-by-city | Community flywheel; low CAC via word of mouth |

---

## Open Questions

- Is the wedge a consumer subscription, a marketplace, or a B2B SaaS?
- How important is the social/community layer vs. solo experiences?
- What does "minimal technology" mean in the actual product? (SMS? Physical mail? Simple web-only?)
- Is there a "Headspace moment" — a category that makes the whole platform legible?
