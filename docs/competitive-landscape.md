# Competitive Landscape — Field Day

> Researched April 2026. No sugarcoating.

---

## Bottom Line Up Front

The space is more fragmented than crowded — but that's not good news in itself. The competitors that have achieved real scale won by occupying different swim lanes: hobbyist communities built over decades, or tourist-occasion transactions. The specific gap we're targeting — **repeatable, locally-rooted, narrative outdoor hunts for families who live somewhere, not visit it** — is real and thinly served.

But the honest risk isn't the named competitors. **It's free content.** A parent who Googles "outdoor scavenger hunt kids" at 9am Saturday finds adequate free printables in under 2 minutes. That's the bar to clear. "Adequate and free" is what kills startups in this category.

---

## The Competitors

### Geocaching — the 800-lb gorilla
**Model:** Freemium. Free app + $30/year premium.
**Scale:** 1.6M active finders, 3.4M active caches in 190+ countries. 25 years of compounding content.
**Their moat:** Cache hiders are the product. Zero content cost. The brand is the category.
**Their weakness:** Zero family onboarding. GPS coordinates, cryptic jargon, no narrative arc. It's a hobby, not a Saturday activity. First-time families bounce and don't return.
**Threat level to us:** High if we ever try to build community content. Low in the "curated family outing" use case.

---

### Let's Roam — closest surface-level comp
**Model:** Per-hunt (~$15–20/person) + annual pass ($89.99).
**Scale:** 400+ cities, 13,000+ reviews, 4.9 stars App Store.
**Their weakness:** Generic tourist content, not neighborhood-native. Reviews flag GPS inaccuracies, outdated clues, thin content. It's a travel activity — not a Saturday habit for people who *live* somewhere.
**Threat level:** Moderate. They have the SEO presence. But they're solving a different problem.

---

### myCityHunt — scale without depth
**Model:** Per-person ~$14.
**Scale:** 5,500+ cities, 167,000+ reviews. Enormous.
**Their weakness:** Breadth over quality. Content is historical trivia and landmark photos. One-shot tourist product, no repeat use design.
**Threat level:** Low. Different use case entirely.

---

### Goosechase / Scavify / Eventzee — corporate tools, not consumer
**Model:** B2B SaaS. Goosechase from $99/event; Scavify ~$1,300/100 people.
**Why it matters:** These are platforms for organizers to build hunts, not families to play them. They've won corporate team-building. No consumer surface.
**Threat level:** Low for consumer. High if we ever pivot to B2B (they already own it).

---

### Urban Adventure Quest — closest family comp
**Model:** Per-team ~$39. One price for 2–5 people.
**Scale:** ~80 US locations. Tripadvisor / GetYourGuide distribution.
**Their weakness:** Tourist destinations only. Static content. 80 locations is not a network. Competing as a travel attraction, not a family weekend product.
**Threat level:** Low to moderate. Right idea, wrong scale and channel.

---

### Free Printables — the actual threat

Sites like Play Party Plan, My Joy-Filled Life, Pinterest — dozens of free downloadable printable scavenger hunts. They dominate Google. Constantly shared in mom Facebook groups.

**How good is the free content?** For a one-time backyard hunt, it is good enough. "Find a bird feather, a smooth rock, something red, a pinecone" scratches the itch.

What it cannot provide: narrative, progression, surprise, local specificity, memorability, any reason to return next week.

**This is the real competitor.** Not Let's Roam. The free printable at playpartyplan.com.

## The Honest Gap

### Where the opportunity is real

| Gap | Why it's open |
|---|---|
| Neighborhood-native content | Every scaled product assumes you're visiting somewhere |
| Repeat-use design | Everything in this space is episodic. Nobody built a family habit loop |
| Ages 4–10 as primary design target | Apps are designed for adults dragging kids. Nothing built from a child's curiosity |
| Narrative/progression across outings | All one-shots. No season-long story |
| Local community layer | Geocaching community is global/anonymous. No neighborhood-level discovery |

---

## What Would Kill Us

**1. Free content is good enough.** If Field Day doesn't deliver something meaningfully better than a free printable, churn is instant.

**2. Content production cost.** Creating locally-specific, narratively compelling hunts for hundreds of neighborhoods is expensive. myCityHunt's 5,500-city library took 15 years. We can't out-content them. We need community-generated or AI-assisted content, or we stay hyper-local intentionally.

**3. Activation friction.** Families need to decide to go outside, open something new, and navigate an unfamiliar product — all before 10am Saturday. Any friction (required download, account wall, confusing pricing) = default to Google = free printable.

**4. SEO entrapment.** Let's Roam and myCityHunt own "outdoor scavenger hunt" search terms. Outranking them organically takes years. Paid acquisition on an $8–12/month product destroys unit economics.

**5. Seasonality.** Austin summers are hostile to outdoor activity. Inherent revenue cliff.

**6. Corporate pivot temptation.** Every product in this space eventually pivots to B2B because the contracts are larger. Staying focused on families requires saying no to obvious money.

---

## Is There an Opportunity?

**Yes. Narrow. Requires a specific bet.**

The defensible position is not "better tourist hunt app." That market is occupied and commoditized.

The defensible position: **a locally-rooted, repeatable, community-native outdoor experience product that gets better the more families in your neighborhood use it.**

That means:
- Content verified by local families, not produced by editorial staff
- Network effects that are neighborhood-level, not global
- Habit-loop design — back every 2 weeks, not once on vacation
- Something physical and tangible that creates memory (a stamp, a field notebook, a collectible)
- One city, deep, before any other city

**The honest risk:** This is a hard and slow business. Geocaching won by being first and building community over 25 years. Engineering that flywheel on purpose, fast, with a startup budget is the real challenge — and there's no obvious shortcut.

---

## What to Watch

- **Geocaching's family UX** — if they ever fix onboarding, they have 25 years of content we can't match
- **Let's Roam expansion into neighborhood content** — they have the distribution
- **AI-generated hunt content** — will arrive and destroy content moats for generic hunts. The non-replaceable value will be local knowledge, community trust, and physical experience design
